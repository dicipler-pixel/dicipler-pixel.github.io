Theorem 4.1 · the operator norm of the Jordan semigroup

Every eigenvalue decays. The states grow anyway.

Inside the invariant plane V = span{x₂, r} the operator is J = [[b, μ], [0, b]], and states evolve as u(t) = e−Jtu(0) = e−bt(x₂ − μt·r, r). The interface coordinate r only decays, but it keeps pushing the shape coordinate x₂. With κ = |μ|/b, the best initial state grows above its starting size exactly when κ > 2. Time runs in units of bt.

Shake the matrix and watch the eigenvalues scatter

For the reduced block the resolvent norm depends only on the distance from the eigenvalue, so every ε-pseudospectrum is exactly a disk: rε = √(ε|μ| + ε²). Small perturbations move the eigenvalue by about √(ε|μ|), far more than ε. The growth test belongs to −J, whose disk is centred at −b; it reaches the right half-plane once ε > εcrit = σmin(J). Here b = 1.

Proposition 5.1 · detuning versus reverse coupling

Two roads into the same defect, at different speeds

Approach the Jordan block two ways and watch the eigenvectors close. Detune the diagonal by δ and the angle shrinks linearly, sin θ = |δ|/√(μ² + δ²). Unfold with a small reverse coupling γ and it shrinks like a square root, sin θ = √(γ/(μ + γ)). At the defect itself the eigenvector frame loses a direction while the invariant plane V stays two-dimensional.

Theorem 2.1 · the exact interface embedding · Section 7

All five coordinates, and the plane that holds the block

The full operator K₅(γ) couples a shape pair (x₁, x₂), a shear pair (s₁, s₂) and an interface coordinate r. The columns for x₂ and r have no entries outside rows x₂ and r, so V = span{x₂, r} is exactly invariant and carries Jγ. The whole five-coordinate norm is therefore never below the reduced one. Section 1 · Figure 1 · what the observations say

The Snake: a kink blamed on a candidate impact

The Galactic Centre Snake is a radio filament about 30′ (roughly 70 pc) long and under 1 pc wide. At its major kink sits G359.13142−0.20005, a compact radio source with an X-ray counterpart. Its discoverers propose, and a 2025 NASA Chandra release restates, that this likely pulsar struck the filament at roughly 500–1000 km/s and distorted its magnetic field. No radio pulses have been detected from it. The confirmed millisecond pulsar PSR J1744−2946 lies in the Sunfish, 49″ due west, and its localization rules out an association with the Snake.

On the current account the Snake is a forced disruption: its kink is attributed to an outside object whose nature and speed are unconfirmed. It is not an observed spontaneous rupture, and archival images cannot show whether a precursor existed. Other readings remain open: filaments lit by relativistic electrons from a nearby source, a stellar trail, or a filament that bends on its own through a kink instability.
