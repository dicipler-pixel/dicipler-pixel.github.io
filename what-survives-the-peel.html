Theorem 8.1 · Theorem 8.2 · Corollary 15.2

Peel an atom and watch each step land on the disk

Read each ionization ratio zₖ = IEₖ₊₁/IEₖ as an impedance. Its reflection Γₖ = (zₖ − 1)/(zₖ + 1) = tanh(½ ln zₖ) sits on the positive real radius of the Smith disk. Steps inside a shell reflect weakly. A step that breaks into a closed shell reflects strongly, and its shell part is fixed by n alone. As the steps add up, the composite walks toward the rim, and its hyperbolic distance from the centre is exactly ln(IEₖ/IE₁).

The shape of the peel finds the family

Hide every element's group. Read only its ladder of ionization energies, then ask its nearest neighbour which group it belongs to. The shape of the ladder, the step sizes ln(IEₖ₊₁/IEₖ), names the group of 95% of the elements at ten steps; the static record of the same numbers, ln IEₖ, names about half. §8.4 · the ridge census · Z ≤ 103

The onion atlas: which closures survive the peel

Peel every element to the end and line the steps up by how many electrons are left. Each row is one element; each column is one electron count. A bright column is a closed shell reached from above: the step into it costs more than its neighbours. Watch the white peel front sweep down through deeper ions. The noble-gas closures 18, 36 and 54 fade; the nickel-like 28 grows with charge. This follows the Coulomb filling order of highly charged ions.

§8.8 · the column rhythm · francium, radium, 119 and 120

Down the column, and where the line breaks

Down each group, ln IE₁ is close to a straight line in the period. Fit the line on periods 2–6 of the alkali metals and it misses francium by 0.55 eV; for the alkaline earths it misses radium by 0.99 eV. The measured columns turn upward at the bottom. The same lines fall 1.55 and 2.14 eV below the relativistic predictions for elements 119 and 120. Where a line under-predicts, the step to that element has slipped a filled inner shell of a new kind, d or f, under the outer electron.

The sign pattern is that of the d-block and lanthanide contractions: a filled d or f shell screens the nucleus poorly, so the electron outside it is held more tightly than the column's trend. Theorems 6.1 and 6.2 · the thinning peel

Thin a metal film and watch its echo cross one circle

A film's reflection is r = Γ ⊕ x, with front surface Γ = (1 − N)/(1 + N) and back-surface echo x = r₁₂e^{4πiNd/λ}. The film reflects more than its bulk exactly when the echo lies outside one circle through the origin. That circle is fixed by the front surface alone; thickness and the back surface only move the echo. Play the peel from 200 nm down to nothing. For gold at 450 nm on glass the echo leaves the circle, so the film brightens before it fades: R = 0.408191 at 200 nm, 0.426742 at its maximum at 58.21 nm, and 0.163082 at 10 nm.

Theorem 6.3 · the thin end is a shunt admittance

The last nanometres: one shunt step from bare glass

To first order in thickness, a film acts on its substrate as a shunt admittance: Y = n_b + y, r = (n₀ − Y)/(n₀ + Y). At the substrate face y = −ikd(N² − n₀²) = Z₀σₛ, which is the familiar thin-sheet formula, exact to first order for any N. At the film's top face the same law reads y = −ikd(N² − n_b²). The usual mistake is to count all of N² as conductance. That is right only when |N²| ≫ n₀², n_b², and for gold it misses 7–35% of the change of r. The miss is all in the phase: the reflectance still agrees to first order.

§6.2 · receiver geometry · the gold metamer

Two colours the eye matches, and gold tells apart

A colour record keeps three numbers of a whole spectrum. Two different spectra can share them exactly: to the CIE 1931 observer under D65 the two halves of the left disc are one colour. Reflect both from gold and the match breaks. The first observation threw away a difference that the next surface was still sensitive to. A neutral filter keeps the match: it is the control. The strongest pair is the top singular vector of CMP, the change a surface can make to what the first receiver could not see.

Theorem 15.1 · Corollaries 15.2 and 15.3 · order of peeling

Peel steps add like velocities

With u ⊕ v = (u + v)/(1 + uv), the reflection of a whole block of removals is the ⊕-sum of its steps: Γₖ→ₗ = Γₖ ⊕ … ⊕ Γₗ₋₁. This is Einstein's law for adding collinear velocities: Γ is a velocity, and τ/2 is a rapidity. As a radar picture, each peel ratio is the frequency ratio of an echo from a mirror approaching at Γc. The ⊕-sum forgets the order of its steps. A thin-film stack only remembers order through the phase between its additions.

Gold (N = 0.4 + 2.3i) and titania (N = 2.4) on glass at 550 nm, in either order. At zero thickness the stack telescopes and order drops out.

Sections 10–13 · VOTKP(K, Ψ) = (topology, loading, geometry, cost)

A record is a vector, not a number

A knot's layers vary independently. One knot, T(3,4), carries five different Hopf charges under five rational-map numerators. Distinct knots with identical HOMFLY, Alexander and Jones polynomials differ in volume. Where a record fails, the next rung resolves it: a longer peel window for atoms, a higher colour for knots.

Numerator loadings

Along T(p,q) the phase of z turns q times and that of w turns p times, so the numerator's phase turns αq + βp times: the Hopf charge Q. T(2,5) at (1,1) and T(3,4) at (1,1) share Q = 7.

§11.5 · §13.7 · the Levine–Tristram signature · ADM-01 and TIME-01

Sweep the phase: walls, windows, and one pair told apart

Turn a phase ω once around the unit circle and record the signature of (1 − ω)V + (1 − ω̄)Vᵀ from the knot's Seifert matrix. It can change only at a wall: a root of the Alexander polynomial on the circle. For a torus knot every representation carries the same sign, so the count of irreducible SU(2) representations is |σ|/2: T(3,4) climbs 0, 1, 2, 3 and back down at its six walls. Then open the 13-crossing pair. The two knots share HOMFLY, Alexander, Jones, three Khovanov homologies, knot Floer homology and classical signature 0. Between two walls, one reads −2 and the other +2.

Section 15.4 · the mass-5 and mass-8 gaps · NUBASE2020

A negative separation energy leaves the disk

No nucleus of mass 5 is bound, and none of mass 8 is stable. On the chart of Theorem 8.1, with z the next separation energy over the first and Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1), every bound peel lies inside the disk. A negative separation energy gives |Γ| > 1. Slide the separation energy through zero and watch the point cross the rim.

The next step is held at its measured value: Sn(⁴He), Sp(⁴He) or Sα(¹²C). The placement says no more than the sign of a separation energy.

Section 15.5 · P-01 and G-02 · Athenodorou & Teper (2020), Table 17 as transcribed

Tight knots against the glueball spectrum

The tight-knot model sets a glueball's mass proportional to the length of a tight knot or link, in order of length. G-02 was registered before the table was used: the k-th shortest object against the k-th lightest state of the model's own J⁺⁺ sector, with a 5% kill on the median miss. Every arm fails. The masses spread faster than the lengths. Try the five one-parameter changes and see what a repair would need; those are post-hoc.

Section 15.7 · Mathur & Sreeraj 2015 · Kogut & Susskind 1975

A loop label is not a particle label

In SU(2) lattice gauge theory, a canonical transformation maps the loop Hilbert space into the Hilbert spaces of hydrogen atoms. A loop of spin j sits at principal quantum number n = 2j + 1. The map is between state spaces, not spectra. On one plaquette the electric energy j(j+1) = (n² − 1)/4 grows, while the Coulomb levels −1/n² close up. On one SU(3) plaquette, the magnetic term connects the empty state to the fundamental loop.

The only affine map that fits n = 1 and n = 2 is E ↦ E − 1. It puts n = 3 at +1, where hydrogen has −1/9.
