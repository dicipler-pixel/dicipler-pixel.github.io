Split an operator, and price its weakest direction

Every real square matrix is a symmetric part plus a skew part, C = S + K. Expanding (S + K)(S − K) − (S − K)(S + K), the squares cancel and [C, Cᵀ] = −2[S, K]: the matrix is normal exactly when its two parts commute. The raw stress Φ = ½‖[C, Cᵀ]‖² = 2‖[S, K]‖² scales as the fourth power and is not a squared sine. Rigidity is a different reading: R_η = 1/(σ_min² + η), the inverse of the weakest transport singular value, regularized, and capped at 1/η.

A band's subspace moves: the metric prices it, the curvature twists it

For a projector P the tangent is off-diagonal, V = PV(I − P) + (I − P)VP, so the tangent pairing is a Gram matrix and the curvature is bounded by the metric: |Ω| ≤ 2√det g. A two-band family saturates it at every point. Here the lower band of d(k) = (sin kₓ, sin k_y, m + 2 − cos kₓ − cos k_y) is carried across the Brillouin zone. Its Chern number is the total curvature over 2π, an integer that changes only where the gap closes.

An integer that holds while the branch guard stays open

For unitaries U and V, the commutator 𝒰 = UVU†V† has determinant one, so the sum of its principal eigenphases is 2π times an integer w. While no eigenvalue reaches −1 (branch guard g_br > 0), w cannot change, |w| ≤ ⌊(d − 1)/2⌋, and the defect obeys δ + g_br² ≤ 4. Clock and shift sit exactly on that edge. Deform V and watch the eigenvalues move while the integer stays put, until one of them touches −1.

Which way, and how well: two readings of one marker

A photon scattered by a trapped atom leaves the atom kicked one way or the other. The marker states' overlap v sets the fringe visibility V = |v|; the best which-way guess sets the distinguishability D = √(1 − V²). For a pure marker D² + V² = 1 and the marker Gram has determinant D². For a warm, mixed marker the sum falls below one, which is Englert's inequality, but the Gram G_ab = Tr(ρU_a†U_b) stays positive at every rank.

An electric field tilts the ground state, and the tilt sets the response

Put a field ℰ on H(ℰ) = H₀ − ℰD. Each transition m contributes a metric piece g⁽ᵐ⁾ = |⟨m|D|0⟩|²/Δₘ², and the lossless polarizability is the same pieces with energy weights: α_s(x) = Σ 2Δₘ³/(Δₘ² − x²) · g⁽ᵐ⁾. Below the first gap the weights are positive, so the silent directions of the response and of the metric are the same. Between resonances the transitions can cancel: a tune-out. A second frequency in the same pole-free interval tells the two apart.

Lower the strengths of fixed transition channels. The metric can only fall, its silent space can only grow, and the rank drops only when the surviving channels stop spanning. The exact oscillator: stiffen the trap and the whole budget moves up

One bound charge in H = p²/2m + mΩ²x²/2 − qℰx. The field only slides the Gaussian ground state, so the fidelity, the metric g = q²/(2mħΩ³) and the polarizability α(ω) = q²/(m(Ω² − ω²)) are all exact, and derived two independent ways. The oscillator strength F = q²ħ²/2m never changes: stiffening the trap carries all of it upward. That is the paper's conserved upward redistribution, realized with the complete sum rule.

Units q = m = ħ = 1; the dilute medium has N/ε₀ = 1/100 and is probed at ω = 1/2.

The energies never move, yet the field metric g = d² sin²ϑ/Δ² does, and with it the response, the permittivity R_opt = 1 + (N/ε₀)α, and the angle light bends at an interface. At an interface: a matching law, a threshold, and two ways to evolve

For the scalar action ½R|q̇|² − V, crossing a flat interface keeps the tangential momentum, so R₁sin²θ₁ = R₂sin²θ₂. A real transmitted angle needs R₁sin²θ₁ ≤ R₂: past that barrier the path turns back. The optical version has a square-root threshold, and on the Smith chart the reflection runs up the real axis to 1 and then around the rim once the wave is evanescent. Separately, a periodic skew generator keeps every norm, while a one-way chain with uniform damping rises and then decays.

The scalar ledger is even to every order; the directed response is not

Take H(t) = H₀ + tE with H₀ symmetric and E skew. Then H(−t) = H(t)ᵀ, the trace of the resolvent is the same at t and −t, and the contour ledger is even. For real positive H₀ it is exact: Z(t) − Z(0) = Σ log(1 + t²ωⱼ²). Even is not constant. And the boundary response built from the same family obeys S_b(−t) = S_b(t)ᵀ: its diagonal is even, but the difference S₁₂ − S₂₁ is odd and moves at first order.

§8.5–8.7 · equations (8.4)–(8.14) · [T] [L]

What survives a wall, and what a boundary measures

At a collision of two modes, H(γ) = σₓ + iγσ_z, each mode's own projector blows up and its left–right overlap falls as √(1 − γ²). The pair's projector stays rank two, and the pair's trace-log stays smooth. At a boundary, eliminating the interior pulls the lost power back to the Hermitian part of the interior operator, I − S†S = 4(WV)†Herm L(WV), with no normality needed. And a thin sheet of dipoles obeys R + T + A = 1 with A ≤ ½: no reflection is not the same as complete absorption.

Needles of light: three questions, one open estimate

A Kakeya set holds a unit segment in every direction; a tube problem asks how thickened segments overlap; a wave estimate controls a coherent sum with its interference. They are related, and they are different. A light packet has a wavelength, a width and a propagation length, and staying a needle over length L needs w² ≳ λL. Wang and Zahl proved the three-dimensional Kakeya dimension; the critical flat-space wave estimate (6.2) is still open, and no finite census bound proves it.
