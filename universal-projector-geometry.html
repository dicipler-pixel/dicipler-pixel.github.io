Section 1 · the redistribution operator

Change can only cross the boundary

A projector Π marks what is presently occupied; a generator Ω is what tries to change it. The part of the change that matters is F(Ω,Π) = (I−Π)ΩΠ + ΠΩ(I−Π): it lives only between the occupied subspace and its complement, its trace is zero, and it vanishes exactly when [Ω,Π] = 0. Turn up the cross-coupling and watch weight leave the occupied block.

Section 1 · redistribution and memory

The same footprint, a different memory

Write the generator in blocks, H = [[A, B], [B†, D]], retained sector first. Eliminating the hidden sector leaves a memory kernel K(t) = B e−itD B† and a source term. Its value at zero time, K(0) = BB†, is fixed by the coupling alone, and it is exactly what redistribution measures. Change the hidden generator D and keep B: the footprint stays put while the memory and the retained trajectory change.

Sections 2 and 6.2 · the metric–curvature bound

Curvature is paid out of the metric

For any two tangents at a projector, |Ω(V,W)| ≤ 2√(g(V,V)g(W,W) − g(V,W)²). Equality holds exactly when the two projected tangent vectors are complex-parallel. Two bands force it, because the complement of a rank-one band is one complex line. Beyond two bands a symmetry can supply it: the kagome lattice at bond phase π/6 saturates on its outer bands with curvature present, and a generic phase leaves slack.

Section 4.1 · the torus anchor

The eigenvalue forgets the direction; the phase keeps it

On the flat torus the twisted operator has paired eigenvalues ±2π√((m+α)²+(n+β)²). Its positive projector carries a phase eiΦ = Z/|Z| that the eigenvalue cannot see. Carry the holonomy around a loop: the magnitude only tells you how far you are from the zero, while the phase records how many times you went around it.

Section 4.2 · local neutrality and the global bound

A neutral point does not make the path free

The potential U(T) = (T² − a²)² + ε has neutral sectors, yet every path from −aI to aI pays O(γ) = ∫(1/N) Tr U dt > 2aε. Squeezing the crossing into a narrow window brings the cost down toward the floor, never below it. A path that moves no faster than one unit per unit time has no such freedom: it must be T = tI, and it pays 16a⁵/15 + 2aε.

Section 4.4 · the noncommutative torus

An integer that needs a large enough failure to commute

Replace the two holonomy numbers by two unitaries U, V and read the relator R = UVU†V†. Its determinant is one, so its eigenphases add to a whole number of turns: the winding w. The branch guard g is the distance of the spectrum from −1, the defect δ how far R is from the identity. Every pair obeys δ + g² ≤ 4, and a nonzero winding needs δ ≥ 16/d².

Sections 2, 4.1 and 5.7 · the Levine–Tristram signature

The record can only step where the polynomial meets the circle

For a knot with Seifert matrix V, the signature of (1−ω)V + (1−ω̄)Vᵀ is an integer step function of ω = e2πiα. It can step only at unit-circle roots of the Alexander polynomial, and a root is a possible step, not a forced one: the square knot has double roots on the circle and never steps. Mirror image reverses the signature, so an amphichiral knot such as the figure-eight carries zero everywhere.

Section 5.3 · chirality forcing

Symmetry is broken by a seed, then amplified

A statically amphichiral substrate carries a zero record, so a persistent distinction needs a chiral substrate or a trajectory that breaks the mirror symmetry. Section 5.6 · the stable trichotomy

Neutral, chiral, or composite

A boundary flow either has no global winding vector (an open flow), or it carries w = (w_α, w_β) ∈ ℤ². Every such vector is exactly one of three kinds: zero (a neutral loop), primitive with gcd = 1 (a chiral loop), or a composite k·w₀. The trichotomy is the statement that composites split into primitive loops under perturbation. That selection needs a stress functional, which the paper leaves open.

Section 6.3 · geometric refraction

Rigidity bends a path that crosses a wall

Give rigidity a dynamical role with the scalar action S = ∫(½R|q̇|² − V)dτ. Across a planar interface the tangential momentum R v sin θ is conserved and so is the energy, so R₁(E−V₁)sin²θ₁ = R₂(E−V₂)sin²θ₂. Beyond the critical angle the path turns back. With R = 1/(s² + η) the transmitted angle never collapses to zero at fixed η.

Sections 6.1 and 8 · the intrinsic quantities

From samples to a projector path

Form a covariance, eigendecompose it, keep the top k directions as a projector Π_k, and follow that projector as a setting moves. Principal angles, persistence P = Tr(Π_k(t)Π_k(t+Δt)) and the effective dimension D_C = 1/IPR are functionals of the projector path alone. Section 8 · projector persistence

Individual projectors fail; the cluster does not
